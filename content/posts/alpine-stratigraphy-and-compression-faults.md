A vertical mountain face is not a static monolith; it is an open ledger of crustal collision. Exposed layers of limestone, shale, and sandstone record millions of years of marine deposition interrupted by tectonic uplift.

When continental plates collide, sedimentary layers are stripped from their basement rock and pushed tens of kilometers inland. In the high alpine ranges, these sheets fold over themselves in colossal recumbent structures known as nappes.

## Brittle Fracture and Ductile Shear

How a rock stratum responds to compressive stress depends primarily on temperature, confining pressure, and strain rate:

1. **Upper crustal brittle deformation:** Near the surface, where temperatures remain below three hundred degrees Celsius, rock cannot flow. Under horizontal stress, it fractures along discrete fault planes. In compression regimes, reverse faults drive older hanging-wall strata upward over younger footwall beds, creating stratigraphic inversions that defy simple superposition.
2. **Deep ductile flow:** At depths exceeding ten kilometers, elevated temperatures and lithostatic pressures soften mineral lattices. Quartz and calcite deform through dislocation creep without losing cohesion. Massive rock bodies fold into sinuous chevron patterns rather than breaking.
3. **Erosional exhumation:** Over tens of millions of years, glacial ice and atmospheric weathering shave away kilometers of overburden. Deep ductile folds rise toward the surface, while late-stage brittle faults crosscut them at high angles.

## Reading the Unconformity

Between distinct sequences of rock, sharp irregular contacts known as angular unconformities mark missing geologic time. A flat layer of Cretaceous limestone sitting directly on steeply tilted Permian schist indicates a prolonged cycle:

- Deposition in an ancient sea basin
- Intense tectonic compression and tilting
- Emergence above sea level and prolonged subaerial erosion
- Renewed subsidence and subsequent sediment accumulation

Every mountain face we observe today is a transient equilibrium between rapid tectonic uplift and relentless gravitational breakdown. The geometry of its strata reveals both the direction of ancient compressive forces and the mechanical limits of solid stone under stress.
